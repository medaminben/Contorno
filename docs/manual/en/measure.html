Measure a hide

From the hide on the table to the surface on the readout.

The surface is only as good as the photo. A few minutes spent on the set-up give the same correct result every day.

  1. Fix the camera above the table, looking straight down. The sensor must be parallel to the table. A camera on a stand or a ceiling mount keeps the distance the same for every hide.

  2. Measure the distance and enter it once. Measure from the table top to the camera's lens and enter it in Set up as Distance to the table, with the Focal length and the sensor. See Set Up.

  3. Lay the hide flat, whole and inside the picture. Smooth out folds and curled edges: a fold hides surface from the camera. Leave a band of table all around the hide. A table whose colour differs from the hide's helps. A mat or cloth under the hide must fill the whole photo.

  4. Light it evenly and take the photo. Avoid strong shadows along the edge and bright reflections. Keep the camera's own photo file: its camera data helps (see Where the scale comes from).

  5. Import the photo. Press Import and choose the file (JPEG, PNG, BMP or TIFF). The busy bar moves for a few seconds; then the outline appears on the right and the readout shows the surface.

  6. Check the outline, then save. Zoom on the edges: the green line should run along the hide's edge, not along a shadow or the table. Press Save to keep the measurement.

What is measured

  • The largest object on the photo. Smaller things on the table, such as dust, labels or a pen, are left out so that the number always names one surface.
  • Holes in the hide that show the table are cut out of the surface, and drawn as their own green rings. Holes smaller than 100 mm² are filled.
  • The surface is the flat projection seen from above: a fold or a wave in the hide is counted as the area it covers, not as the leather inside it.
  • Objects that touch each other are one object.

Check the scale with a known object

Put a flat object of known size on the table, about as large as a hide, for example a board of 1000 × 1000 mm (1.000 m²), photograph it like a hide and import it. Do not use a small object: the outline lies about one pixel outside the edge, which weighs more on a small object, and a calibration on it would make every hide read too low. If the readout differs, check the Distance to the table first: it is the value most often wrong.

To calibrate, change Distance to the table in Set up until the known object reads right, and keep that distance. The surface changes with the square of the distance: if the readout is 2 % too high, lower the distance by about 1 %. This also corrects the small error of treating the lens as a pinhole (see Reference). Do it again whenever the camera, the lens or the zoom changes.
